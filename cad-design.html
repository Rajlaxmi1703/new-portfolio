Module 03

CAD Design

My 7-day learning plan through Autodesk Fusion 360 — from basic sketching to full 3D modeling, assemblies, and a final design project.

Rajlaxmi Rede
SSGMCE, Shegaon
2025
Fusion 360
7 Days Covered

About This Module

CAD Design is about turning ideas into precise, buildable 3D models. In this module I am learning Autodesk Fusion 360 — a professional CAD/CAM/CAE tool — following a structured 7-day plan that takes me from the very basics of sketching to designing a complete final project.

My own model screenshots for each day are still in progress and will be added here once ready — for now, this page tracks my learning plan, what I'm practising, and what each day is meant to teach me.

DAY
1
Day 1 — Introduction & Interface
CAD/CAM/CAE Basics · Workspace · Navigation · First Sketch
What is Fusion 360 — CAD, CAM, and CAE basics
Workspace overview — Design, Solid, Surface
Navigation — zoom, pan, orbit
Creating my first sketch
Practice
  • Draw basic shapes — rectangle, circle, line

💡 What I'll learn: How to get comfortable navigating the Fusion 360 interface and understand the difference between the Design, Solid, and Surface workspaces before touching any real modeling.

DAY
2
Day 2 — Sketching (2D Basics)
Sketch Tools · Constraints · Dimensions
Sketch tools — Line, Circle, Arc, Rectangle
Constraints — Coincident, Parallel, Perpendicular
Dimensions — locking down exact sizes
Practice
  • Draw a fully constrained sketch (no blue lines left)

💡 What I'll learn: Constraints are what make a sketch reliable — a fully constrained sketch (all lines turn black, not blue) means the shape won't accidentally change later. This is the foundation for accurate 3D models.

DAY
3
Day 3 — Advanced Sketch Practice
Offset · Trim · Extend · Mirror · Pattern
Offset, Trim, and Extend tools
Mirror and Pattern for symmetric/repeated shapes
Building complex sketches from simple ones
Practice
  • Draw a simple gear outline
  • Draw a bracket shape

💡 What I'll learn: How to combine multiple sketch tools together — Mirror and Pattern especially save time on symmetric parts like gears, instead of drawing every tooth by hand.

DAY
4
Day 4 — 3D Modeling (Core Day 🚀)
Extrude · Revolve · Fillet · Chamfer
Converting a 2D sketch into a 3D body with Extrude
Revolve — spinning a sketch profile into a solid
Modify tools — Fillet (rounded edges) and Chamfer (beveled edges)
Practice
  • Make a cylinder
  • Make a bolt-like shape

💡 What I'll learn: This is the core day — going from flat 2D sketches to real 3D solids. Extrude pushes a shape into depth, Revolve spins it around an axis (perfect for bolts and cylindrical parts), and Fillet/Chamfer make edges look manufactured rather than blocky.

DAY
5
Day 5 — Assembly & Motion
Components vs Bodies · Joint Tool · Motion Study
Understanding Components vs Bodies
Joint tool — connecting parts so they move correctly
Motion study — simulating how an assembly moves
Practice
  • Create a simple hinge mechanism

💡 What I'll learn: A Body is a single solid shape, while a Component can hold multiple bodies and move independently — this distinction matters once parts need to interact, like a hinge that actually rotates using a Joint.

DAY
6
Day 6 — Engineering Practice
Spur Gear · Plate with Holes · Measure & Inspect
Creating real parts — a basic spur gear
Designing a plate with holes
Measure tool — checking exact dimensions
Inspect tool — verifying model accuracy
Practice
  • Design a laser-cut plate

💡 What I'll learn: Moving from "just modeling" to real engineering practice — using Measure and Inspect to verify a design is actually accurate enough to manufacture, not just visually correct.

DAY
7
Day 7 — Final Project 🎯
Combining Everything I Learned
Combining sketching, 3D modeling, and assembly skills
Designing a complete, functional object from scratch
Project Ideas
  • Phone stand
  • Power button assembly
  • Simple clamp
  • Key holder (laser cutting)

💡 What I'll learn: This is where everything comes together — picking a real, useful object and designing it end-to-end using every skill from the past 6 days.

My Overall Reflection

CAD Design is teaching me to think in a completely different way — turning ideas into precise, measurable, buildable models instead of just sketches on paper. Following this 7-day plan gives me a clear path from the basics of Fusion 360's interface all the way to designing a real final project.

My own model screenshots are still a work in progress. As I complete each day's practice in Fusion 360, I'll update this page with my actual sketches and models.
